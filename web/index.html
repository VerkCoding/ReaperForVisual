ReaperForVisual

Where every part of your mix sits, as you hear it. Left or right is where the part is heard, measured from its meters after every pan, send and bus on its way. Low or high is its spectral centre, read from its audio files: 40 Hz at the floor, 12 kHz at the top (the post at the back right shows the scale). Near and big is loud compared with the whole mix (the rings mark 0, −10, −20 and −30 dB). Wide means its left and right have little in common. A cloud is a return, such as a reverb; a faint shell is a bus or group shown with its tracks inside. Hollow means muted, or cut by a solo, and drawn where it was last heard.

Stopped, the stage shows the edit cursor. Where the song has played, each part stands where its meter put it there, through the faders and pans as they are now. Elsewhere it is worked out from its files, with its effects as measured where it played. Play the song once, and the stage when stopped is as exact as while playing. What playing measured is kept for your next session in REAPER's resource folder (Data/ReaperForVisual).

The first list at the top shows a section of the song instead of the cursor: its average stage, and how much of it was measured while it played. Sections are your regions (one that covers the whole song, made for rendering, is left out), else markers whose name starts with #. The second list chooses where sections come from.

Height and width are read before each track's effects, so an EQ or a widener on a track is not seen. "?" marks a part whose sound does not come from audio files (a virtual instrument, a live input). Hover over a part for its numbers and the meaning of its marks. Nothing in your project is changed.
